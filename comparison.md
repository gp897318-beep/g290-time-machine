# Candidate comparison — Funniest Time Machine Warning Label

Judge brief (in order): (1) must be a time-machine warning label;
(2) turns a real hazard (paradox, duplicate self, stranded ancestor)
into the joke instead of naming it flatly; (3) saves the surprise for
the last few words; (4) keeps a straight face, phrased like a genuine
safety notice; (5) wastes no words. Limit: 120 characters, one line.

| # | Chars | Line |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | 104 | WARNING: Do not kill your grandfather. Management is not responsible for passengers who were never born. |
| 2 | 112 | NOTICE: Passengers who prevent their own birth will be charged a full no-show fee and escorted off the timeline. |
| 3 | 106 | WARNING: If you see yourself boarding this machine, do not wave. He is not waving back. He is warning you. |
| 4 | 107 | NOTICE: In the event of a paradox, remain calm, do not exist, and await further instructions from yourself. |
| 5 | 99 | WARNING: Do not step on butterflies. One extinction voids the warranty and your entire family tree. |
| 6 | 113 | CAUTION: This machine returns you to the exact moment you left, which is why your past self is already suing you. |
| 7 | 110 | WARNING: Never meet your past self. He has had years to plan this conversation, and you have had five minutes. |
| 8 | 107 | WARNING: Do not bring your future self home for dinner. He eats twice as much and knows all your passwords. |

## Verdict

**Chosen: #1.** The grandfather paradox is the most instantly recognisable
time-travel hazard, and the joke is not the paradox itself but the
corporate disclaimer about it: management disclaiming responsibility for
"passengers who were never born" is the punchline, and it lands in the
last three words. The whole line reads like a real posted notice, and at
104 characters nothing in it is dead weight.

Runner-up: #2 — the no-show fee for preventing your own birth is the same
deadpan register, but its punchline ("escorted off the timeline") is a
weaker second beat than #1's, and it spends more words getting there.

- #3: good twist ("He is warning you") but the setup is long and the joke
  is about waving, not about a hazard.
- #4: "do not exist" is a strong absurd instruction, but it names the
  paradox flatly up front (criterion 2) instead of turning it into a joke.
- #5: butterfly effect as a warranty void is fresh and tight, but the
  punchline is a single image rather than a twist.
- #6: funny premise, but "suing you" is a generic payoff and the line is
  the longest of the set.
- #7: the "five minutes" asymmetry is nice, yet the laugh is about
  conversation prep, not a time-travel hazard.
- #8: warm and likeable, but dinner-table humour, not a warning label —
  weakest fit for criterion 1.
